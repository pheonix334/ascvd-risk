What is ASCVD risk?

ASCVD stands for Atherosclerotic Cardiovascular Disease — plaque buildup in the arteries that can lead to heart attack, stroke, or peripheral artery disease. A "10-year ASCVD risk" estimate answers a specific question: based on your current risk factors, what's the statistical chance of a first major cardiovascular event in the next decade?

Doctors use this kind of estimate, alongside the full clinical picture, to help decide whether lifestyle changes alone are appropriate or whether medication (like a statin) should be part of the conversation.

How this calculator works

This tool uses the same core risk factors as the official ACC/AHA Pooled Cohort Equations — age, sex, total and HDL cholesterol, systolic blood pressure (and whether it's treated), smoking status, and diabetes — and combines them into a transparent point total. That total places you into one of four general risk tiers, matching the same tier boundaries used in clinical guidelines.

What this tool doesn't do is reproduce the exact statistical formula (the Pooled Cohort Equations use complex race- and sex-specific regression coefficients derived from large research cohorts). For that precise percentage, use the official calculator linked above — this tool is meant to give you a clear, honest sense of where you likely stand and what's driving it.

Factors that drive ASCVD risk

📅
Age
The single biggest driver — risk rises steeply through your 50s, 60s, and beyond.
💉
Blood pressure
Higher systolic pressure, especially if untreated, meaningfully raises risk.
🥦
Cholesterol
High total cholesterol combined with low HDL ("good" cholesterol) raises risk.
🚬
Smoking
One of the most modifiable major risk factors — quitting has an outsized benefit.
🧭
Diabetes
Substantially raises cardiovascular risk independent of other factors.
🧬
Family history & genetics
Not in this calculator's formula, but a doctor will factor it into your overall picture.

In risk models, a hard ASCVD event typically means a first non-fatal heart attack, death from coronary heart disease, or a fatal or non-fatal stroke — the major, unambiguous cardiovascular events researchers track.

The modifiable factors are the ones worth focusing on: quitting smoking, managing blood pressure, improving cholesterol (through diet, exercise, or medication), and managing blood sugar if you have diabetes. Each of these has strong evidence behind it for reducing cardiovascular risk over time.
